At the start of 2026, I'd been trying to keep tabs on postings by John Seymour, a Portsmouth/Gosport based promoter who runs the [*Underground Overground* stage](https://www.facebook.com/people/Underground-Overground-Stage/61586042694908/#), to see if that stage was calling for Wickham Festival. The first mention I saw was when the line up was being announced, but John had also been posting about the [Gosport Sunset Festival](https://gosportsunsetfestival.co.uk/), so I took a punt and made a late approach to get a slot with Ryde-based folk duo, Berlingo Flick, (Jess Ong and Al Watson), who I'd seen a couple of times at the Solent, and who had piqued my interest further with a Cowes based song, *The Last Duel*, they had sung at the Kashmir Fringe earlier in the year.

Jess and Al were up for accompanying tales, as well as turn taking with narrative songs, so I made the pitch, though I think as a result of my lack of admin skills getting the billing sorted properly, we missed getting on the poster.

![](/images/gosport_sunset_festival_2026.jpg)

But we did get the slot — opening on the Sunday.

After a chat and an informal, and partial, run through, we had the forty minutes we needed. Three songs and a couple of accompanied tales — *England's Dreaming* and the *Tale of John the Painter*. Then it was off to the Solent Inn for a couple of slots graciously extended to us by Jim, for a run through...

![](/images/with_berlingo_flick_solent_july_2026.jpg)

It was an early-ish ferry on the day itself: even though the trip over to Gosport is a five minute ferry from Portsmouth Harbour, it's a forty minute drive round.

![](/images/gosport_sunset_festival_2026.jpg)

The festival itself was in a small, fenced off park in Gosport. The gates hadn't opened when we arrived, and even though we were due on at 11, the security guard on the gate wouldn't let us in till the gates opened: at 11. So we walked round the park — fenced all the way and the railings just a little too hight to climb over without doing ourselves an injury...but then we found the emergency exit gate, and popped in that way.

Inside the festival arena — a small park but well laid out — the main tent, the Underground Overground tent, and a disco tent, along with various food stalls and a bar. Our stage was a tiny outdoor stage, next to the main tent.

![](/images/with_berlingo_flick_gosport_waiting.jpg)

With a start time of 11, and gates at 11, it was likely we weren't going to have a huge crowd — though Jess had family over from the Island, which guaranteed us some audience at least — but the early start worked in out favour: the *other* tents were quiet (the main stage next door would fire its speakers up just after we'd finished), which meant there was at least a chance folk could hear the words of the stories I told...

The stage layout itself was compact: Jess and Al took to cocktail seats at the back, I had a mic middle front. This worked well for the accompaniment — though it would have be nice to be able to make eye contact in case I messed up my cues! — and I nipped off to the side when Jess and Al did the songs ("William Taylor", "The Last Duel", and "The Tipton Slasher").

![](/images/with_berlingo_flick_gosport_the_duel.jpg)

All in all, a great day out:-)
