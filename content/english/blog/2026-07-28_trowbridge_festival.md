By chance, whilst poking around looking for small summer festivals, I came across [Trowbridge Festival](https://www.trowbridgefestival.co.uk/), over Bath way, which happened to have *Otway*, *Three Daft Monkeys*. *Merry Hell*, and a host of other interesting looking things on the bill.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_lineup.jpg)

I sent in a very late expression of interest, wondering if there might be a chance of tellin' some tales, and got an email back from Lizzy (Toplicean) saying that there just might be...

I explained how my focus was on telling tales primarily to adults, though the stories would also be appropriate all ages, and I got a slot on the Saturday and the Sunday in the breakfast tent, which was repurposed to a sessions tent after the breakfast service.

Although I got in way too late to make the poster or the t-shirt, I did get a listing on the website.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_website.png)

We'd originally planned to go up on the Friday, but with the last of my Ventnor Fringe shows that afternoon, decided it might be too much of rush. So we went up on the Saturday, a first trek to a mainland festival where we'd get to go in via the production entrance.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_which_way.jpg)

Back in the day, Trowbridge (Folk) Festival seems to have been one of the larger and more ambitious folk festivals, but now, reinvented and starting again, it fills just a single field: the main stage on the bottom, a second stage (the Pump) on the top edge, a bar (with a stage) on one side, and a small kids area — plus the breakfast/session tent — on the other.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_bar.jpg)

There were no places to access, but the performer wrist band did get us in and out of the side gate and the staff/performers' camping area.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_wristbands.jpg)

I'd made a few small flyers to hand around, and popped a few in the bar an on the merch desk, but when it came time to do the first set, Emma couldn't help laughing...

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_golden_ticket.jpg)

...an audience of kids...

It was always going to be that way of course, not least because of the positioning of the tent, but any audience is a good audience, so I made a start. I'd deliberately opted for tales that were kid friendly — *Persimmon Tree* and *Gwion Bach* — and the slot was a short one, but I didn't realise how much fun it would be...

On the Sunday, with an open mic opening things up again in the Pump tent, I managed to get a short slot squeezed in at the end.

![](/images/trowbridge_2026_pump.jpg)

As the session went on, it started to over-run more and more. Rather than cut anyone short and reduce their song allocations, a cursory mention of how things were over-running and not to go on too much didn't stop folk adding verses to their songs, giving the full commentary, and even adding in a round of "Happy Birthday".

My (short) slot was cut short, and I geva. cut down version of the cut-down version of the *Wish Ring*. But it brought story to a few more people and I had some good comments after, as well as being recognised from that tent a couple of festivals later. So well worth it...

The storytelling slot that day was quieter than the day before — it was later in the day, and folk were back to their tents for tea or to pack away for an early exit — which meant a couple of separate small, but appreciative, adult audiences. In a way, I missed the opportunity of telling to a family audience but that would re-present itself a few days later at Wickham.

In all, a fabulous festival, all in one compact field, family friendly, good beer (with performers' discount:-) and we'd be really keen to back there... and maybe do a set in the bar?;-)
